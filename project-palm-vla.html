Palm VLA Research Platform

A training pipeline for a Vision-Language-Action model that combines realistic palm simulation, digital twin calibration, teleoperation, and synthetic data generation.

Focus: Blender simulation, Isaac Sim, rigid body physics, robotic arm calibration, and task execution for spraying and trimming palm fronds.

Collaborators: University of Hawaii at Manoa climber team, University of Michigan climber team, and the end effector team supporting crown-level deployment.

Project Workflow

  1. We built a realistic palm tree simulation in Blender, then added rigid body physics and frond colliders while fine-tuning mass and stiffness for lifelike interaction testing.
  2. We CAD-designed and 3D-printed a custom robotic arm, exported the system into Isaac Sim, and calibrated the physical arm to mirror its digital twin.
  3. By teleoperating the physical arm, we drove the simulation and collected high-quality human-expert demonstrations for the foundational training dataset.
  4. We scaled training with a synthetic data generation pipeline that randomized arm positioning and environments while autonomously executing spraying and trimming tasks.
  5. We then extended the workflow into the field with climber-team partners to mount the arm for operation at the crown of the palms.

Deployment Context

The end effector team’s spraying and trimming tooling was used to service the crown and flowers while preserving the same motion targets represented in simulation.

This made it possible to move from a controlled digital environment into a deployment workflow that reflects the real constraints of palm maintenance.
